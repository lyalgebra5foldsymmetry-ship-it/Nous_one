# Executive Research Brief for Chloe Tully  
## Formalizing Scaffold-Mediated Causal Continuity, Reservoir Circulation, Dependency Reorganization, Budding, and the Candidate LIMEN Viability Transition

### Purpose of this brief

Chloe,

Our discussions have substantially changed the origin-of-life problem I am trying to formulate in LIMEN. Your causal-rate contrast was important to this development because it forced us to distinguish something that merely persists from something whose presence or prior occurrence actually changes what becomes possible afterward.

Subsequent work has sharpened the problem further. We now think that a relevant prebiotic field may contain not only environmental scaffolds that enable incomplete chemistry, but—where environmental variability makes temporal buffering important—**reservoir functions** capable of carrying usable causal capacity across changing conditions and returning it to active circulation.

Reservoir function should not be treated as a mandatory rung through which every possible route to life must pass. A sufficiently continuous environment, recurrent regeneration, transport, or another mechanism may preserve temporal availability without a distinct reservoir. The more general problem is continuity of usable causal capacity across changing dependency conditions. Reservoir dynamics are one potentially powerful physical solution to that problem.

That addition nevertheless changes the mathematical problem enough that I would like to treat the following as the new baseline.

The central question is:

> **Can a nonliving chemical field, supported by environmental scaffolding and—where physically relevant—reservoir-mediated continuity, undergo cumulative, physically embodied reorganization until recurrent chemical relations begin participating in renewal of the very conditions that allow those relations to continue?**

I am not asking you to validate the chemistry, assume that this mechanism historically produced life, or formalize a threshold we have already decided must exist.

The task should remain adversarial.

The main questions are:

1. Is the proposed causal architecture mathematically coherent?
2. What is the minimum formal structure needed to represent it?
3. Can scaffold-mediated causal continuity be distinguished from ordinary persistence or ordinary environmental change?
4. Where reservoir processes occur, can reservoir-mediated continuity be distinguished from stockpiling, sink formation, or deferred depletion?
5. Can temporally separated chemical processes become effectively co-available through retention, recurrence, transport, regeneration, or reservoir-mediated return?
6. Can dependency provenance shift without assuming a preexisting organismal boundary?
7. Can recurrent chemistry progressively participate in restoring reservoirs, scaffolds, gradients, or other enabling conditions without presupposing biological regulation?
8. Can reciprocal self-maintenance emerge without presupposing replication, heredity, natural selection, agency, orientation, or biological function?
9. Does a genuine qualitative transition appear at all?
10. Is there instead only a smooth increase in persistence, buffering, recurrence, or reservoir stock?
11. If a threshold appears, is it an intrinsic dynamical transition, an operational maintenance boundary, or both?

The notation below is conceptual scaffolding, not a preferred mathematical formalism. Please replace it wherever another treatment is cleaner.
